# Direct messages and groups

**For members.** Besides channels, you can hold private conversations with one person (a
**direct conversation**) or with a small group. Nothing is shared in these conversations until
the other person agrees.

> **On by default, not independently reviewed.** Direct conversations and groups are on for
> this server unless the operator switched them off. They are implemented and tested, but no
> independent security or privacy review has been made. See [Known limitations](../known-limitations.md).

## Ask someone to talk

Choose **Start a conversation** in the sidebar, or open a person's profile and choose
**Request conversation**. Write an optional note of up to 500 characters and send the request.
The person sees it under **Messages and requests**.

Until they accept, there is no history, no file exchange and no call between you. You can see
that a request is **Request sent** and withdraw it at any time with **Withdraw request**.

## Answer a request

Open **Messages and requests** in the sidebar. A number shows how many requests are waiting.
Each request shows who sent it and its note, with two choices:

- **Accept request** — starts the conversation, which then appears under **Messages**.
- **Decline request** — refuses it. The sender is told it was declined, not why, and by default
  must wait 24 hours before asking again.

Accepting is your decision alone. Someone who asks you cannot accept on your behalf, and a
request sent in both directions still needs each of you to accept.

### Control who may ask you

**Allow message requests** in **Your preferences** decides whether other members can send you new
requests. Turn it off to stop others from sending you new requests.

The server also limits how many requests one account may have pending and how many it may start
in a day, so a flood of requests cannot fill a person's inbox. Those limits are the server's
defaults.

## Talk in a direct conversation

Once accepted, a direct conversation works like a channel: write, reply, react, pin, edit and
delete your own messages, and attach files. Search inside it stays inside that conversation.
You can delete your own messages in a direct conversation at any time.

## Block someone

Open the person's profile and choose **Block person**. Blocking works in both directions:

- While you block someone, they cannot send you requests or messages, and you cannot send them any.
- An existing direct conversation becomes read-only for both of you. You can still delete your own
  messages.
- Blocking does not remove either person from a shared community channel.

Unblock with **Unblock person**, from your profile or from **Blocked people**. Unblocking does not
resend anything that was not sent while the block was in place.

**Report and block** does both at once. The report gives the server's reviewers only the messages
you selected; see [Privacy and safety](privacy-and-safety.md#reporting-someone).

## Groups

A group is a conversation among several people. Start one with **Create group** or **Start a group**, then invite
people with **Invite someone**.

- An invitation names the group. The person joins only when they **accept** it. Nobody is added
  without consenting.
- **Read earlier history is part of the invitation.** An accepted member can read the group's
  history from before they joined. The invitation says so before it is accepted, so a person
  who does not want that can decline.
- A group's messages, reactions, pins, search and files work the same way as a direct
  conversation.
- To leave, choose **Leave**. A group whose last member leaves is removed.
- A group can hand its ownership to another member with **Make owner**, for example before you
  leave.

## When someone leaves the server

If an account is removed, its pending requests are cancelled and its introductions are cleared.
Conversations it had accepted remain readable by the members who remain. The removed person's
own messages may be removed with the account; the administrator's choice is described in
[Administrators](administrators.md#people).
