# Getting started

**For members.** This chapter takes you from an invitation to your first message.

## 1. Accept an invitation

An administrator creates an invitation and sends you a link. Each link creates one account, and
it expires: invitations last between one hour and seven days, as the administrator chose. If a
link says it is expired or already used, ask for a new one. Nobody can give you access through
any other route, because the server has no sign-up page.

Some invitations also grant a role — a set of extra abilities such as moderating a channel. The
invitation shows what it will grant. An empty selection means you are an ordinary member.

## 2. Choose a username and password

You choose your own:

- **Username:** 3 to 32 letters, numbers or underscores, using ASCII characters only. Other
  members see it, and you sign in with it.
- **Password:** 12 to 72 bytes. Longer phrases are better than short complicated strings; the
  server stores only a hash of it, but the password is still the only key to your account.

You can change your display name and colour later in **Your preferences → Profile**. Your
username stays the same.

## 3. Sign in

Open the server's address in a browser — for example `https://chat.example.org` — and sign in
with your username and password. If you have turned on two-factor sign-in, you are asked for a
code next; see [Your account and security](account-and-security.md).

Sign-in sessions end when you sign out, when your password changes, or when an administrator
signs you out everywhere. Use **Sign out** at the bottom of the sidebar on a shared computer.

## 4. Find your way around

The screen has three working areas.

**The sidebar (left)** shows **Your community**, the server name, and the channels, grouped in
categories. Under the channel list you will find:

- **Jump to a channel** — opens a search of channels by name, topic or category. Shortcut:
  **Ctrl/⌘ + K**. Unread channels and mentions are listed first. Use the arrow keys and Enter.
- **Messages and requests** — your direct conversations and any requests waiting for you. A
  number shows requests you still have to answer. See [Direct messages and groups](direct-messages-and-groups.md).
- **Start a conversation** — asks someone for a private conversation.
- **Manage server** — visible only to people with administrative abilities.
- **Your data export**, **Your preferences** (the gear) and **Sign out**, at the foot of the sidebar.

**The conversation (centre)** shows the messages of the channel or conversation you have open,
with the composer at the bottom. The header holds the channel's **Threads in this channel**,
**Pinned messages**, **Search messages** and **Show members** controls. Shortcut for message
search: **Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + K**.

**The members panel (right)** lists everyone on the server and whether they are online. Select a
name to open that person's profile.

On a phone, the channel list and the members panel open from buttons in the header instead of
staying on screen; the **Open channels** button is at the top left.

## 5. Send your first message

Open a text channel from the sidebar, click in the composer, type, and press **Enter**. On a
computer, **Shift + Enter** starts a new line without sending. On a touch screen, **Enter** starts
a new line and **Ctrl/⌘ + Enter** sends, so the on-screen keyboard does not send by accident.

If a send cannot finish, your text is kept — see [Messages](messages.md#when-a-message-does-not-send).

## 6. Set up the things that protect you

Do these in your first session:

1. **Turn on two-factor sign-in** in **Your preferences → Account** and save the ten recovery
   codes somewhere private. Without them, a lost phone can lock you out for good. See
   [Your account and security](account-and-security.md).
2. **Choose your notification settings** in **Your preferences → Notifications**, so you hear
   about what matters and not everything. See [Notifications and preferences](notifications-and-preferences.md).
3. **Test your microphone** in **Your preferences → Audio** before you join a call. See
   [Voice and screen sharing](voice-and-screen-sharing.md).

## Install it like an app

BrrpBrrp is a web app that can be installed. In Chrome, Edge or Safari on a computer, use the
browser's **Install** or **Add to Dock** option from the address bar or menu. On an iPhone or
iPad, use Safari's **Share → Add to Home Screen**, and open the installed app once before you turn
on notifications. An installed app still needs the server to be reachable; it does not keep
messages for offline reading.
